\(\pi = \sqrt{6 \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2}}\)

Called Basel's Sequence Identity, it's a rather simple sequence while still being equal to π and being elegant. It initially was a problem from Basel in the 1600's, but Euler solved it in 1734.

\(\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x} = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1 - \frac{x^2}{n^2}\right)\)

Developped by Euler in the 1730's - 1740's, this behemoth uses trigonometric functions and infinite products to make a juste 1 constant : π.

\(\frac{\pi}{2} = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n}{2n-1} \cdot \frac{2n}{2n+1}\)

Named Wallis' Product, it's an infinite product through ratios of consecutive even and odd numbers. It's one of the earliest known infinite product formulas for π, discovered by John Wallis in the 17th century.
